# Norvana Global & Resale Sourcing Policy R0

**Status:** sourcing qualification candidate  
**Goal:** find lawful, authentic and profitable inventory while keeping fixed cost and prepaid inventory as close to $0 as practical.

## Sourcing lanes

### A. Overseas generic/private-label

Examples of discovery venues may include large B2B marketplaces, manufacturer directories, trade fairs and direct factories.

Preferred economics:

- free buyer account;
- low or no MOQ;
- sample ordering;
- supplier-direct fulfillment where practical;
- transparent shipping;
- protected payment;
- independent inspection option;
- API/feed or manageable catalog synchronization.

Supplier marketplace badges are evidence inputs, not automatic approval.

### B. Overseas authentic brand-name

Higher-risk lane.

A supplier offering unusually cheap branded merchandise must provide evidence that can support authenticity and lawful resale/import.

Possible evidence includes:

- brand authorization or distributor letter;
- invoices showing chain of custody;
- manufacturer/distributor identity;
- verifiable UPC/GTIN/serial data where relevant;
- product photographs and packaging;
- country/market of intended sale;
- customs/import documentation where relevant;
- warranty status;
- proof goods are not materially different in a way that creates U.S. trademark/import restrictions.

A low price is not proof of a legitimate deal.

### C. U.S. official liquidation / overstock

Prefer direct retailer/manufacturer channels and itemized manifests when available.

Candidate conditions may include:

- new;
- shelf-pull;
- seasonal overstock;
- refurbished;
- customer return;
- salvage.

Condition must be disclosed accurately to Norvana customers.

### D. Thrift / secondhand / auction

Potential sources include nonprofit auction marketplaces, estate auctions, resale platforms and local closeouts.

Use this lane selectively for products with enough spread after:

- shipping;
- authentication;
- cleaning/testing;
- missing parts;
- condition variance;
- returns;
- slower sell-through.

Do not treat one-off thrift inventory like a scalable dropship supplier.

## Authenticity and IP rules

Norvana must not knowingly source counterfeit goods.

For brand-name goods, the candidate truth state begins as:

`AUTHENTICITY_UNVERIFIED`

It may advance only with evidence.

Suggested states:

- AUTHENTICITY_UNVERIFIED
- PROVENANCE_PARTIAL
- AUTHENTICITY_SUPPORTED
- AUTHORIZED_CHANNEL_CONFIRMED
- GRAY_MARKET_REVIEW_REQUIRED
- IMPORT_RESTRICTED
- REJECTED_COUNTERFEIT_RISK

A marketplace verification badge does not prove that a particular branded product is authorized or genuine.

## Gray-market rule

Genuine goods sourced outside the brand's normal U.S. distribution channel can still create trademark/import issues.

Before importing branded goods for resale, Norvana should check whether:

- the trademark is subject to CBP gray-market protection;
- the foreign and U.S. goods are materially different;
- U.S.-specific labeling, safety, certification or warranty requirements apply;
- the supplier actually has lawful title to the goods.

When uncertainty is material, route to legal/customs review rather than guessing.

## Foreign-company diligence

Before commercial approval of an overseas partner, collect and evaluate:

- legal company name;
- registration/jurisdiction;
- years operating;
- physical address;
- beneficial ownership when reasonably available;
- sanctions/restricted-party screening;
- export history;
- references/reviews;
- dispute history;
- payment terms;
- sample quality;
- factory/trader status;
- quality certificates relevant to the product;
- inspection rights;
- Incoterms;
- shipping method;
- returns/defect policy.

For higher-value relationships, use independent company verification/background checking where justified.

## Profitability gate

Never rank opportunities on headline purchase price alone.

Calculate expected landed economics:

- unit acquisition price;
- MOQ/case/pallet requirement;
- inbound shipping;
- duty/tariff;
- customs broker/entry fees when relevant;
- inspection/authentication;
- damaged/missing unit allowance;
- payment/platform fees;
- Norvana fulfillment cost;
- customer return allowance;
- expected sale price;
- expected sell-through time;
- working capital tied up;
- expected contribution margin and dollars.

For liquidation/thrift lots, model a downside case as well as an expected case.

## Drop-ship preference

When two qualified sources are materially similar, prefer the source that:

- requires less prepaid inventory;
- has lower fixed cost;
- can fulfill directly;
- exposes reliable inventory state;
- provides tracking;
- supports returns;
- has shorter delivery time;
- has a usable API/feed;
- gives Norvana better evidence/provenance.

## Test-before-scale rule

For new overseas or brand-name suppliers:

1. research;
2. collect provenance;
3. order sample/test lot when economical;
4. inspect/authenticate;
5. test delivery and returns;
6. verify margin;
7. approve limited pilot;
8. scale only after evidence supports it.

## Agent authority

A sourcing agent may research and recommend.

It may not autonomously:

- spend Norvana funds;
- bid on auctions;
- commit to MOQ/contract;
- import restricted goods;
- assert brand authorization;
- publish inventory;
- activate auto-fulfillment.

Consequential actions require explicit Norvana authority and receipts.

## Candidate scorecard

Each candidate should report:

- source name and country;
- lane;
- product/category;
- brand if any;
- authenticity/provenance status;
- seller/company diligence status;
- buyer signup cost;
- MOQ;
- product cost;
- estimated landed cost;
- estimated resale range;
- expected gross/contribution margin;
- shipping time;
- fulfillment model;
- return policy;
- integration/API capability;
- U.S. import/IP concerns;
- confidence;
- evidence URLs/documents;
- recommended next action.

## Example source classes worth watching

- official retailer/manufacturer liquidation marketplaces;
- authenticated/qualified wholesale distributors;
- overseas B2B marketplaces with protected payment and audited suppliers;
- nonprofit/estate auction marketplaces;
- brand closeouts;
- refurbishment channels;
- local business liquidations.

Discovery is not approval.
